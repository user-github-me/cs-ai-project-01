# Blue Impulse — record your narration

Use these five scripts to record your own voice for the tutorial videos. Each PDF has an identical plain-text version. The 368 narration paragraphs preserve the current spoken wording exactly; teaching outlines, visual directions and prerequisite lists are excluded.

**Read only the narration paragraphs. Do not read the title, section headings, section numbers, timestamps or bracketed recording notes.** Spoken introductions, questions and closing credits within the narration paragraphs are part of the script.

## Record naturally

- Record one complete video per audio file, following its sections in order. The section timestamps refer to the original ten-minute edit; do not race to match them or add silence to force a ten-minute recording. The visuals and subtitles can be retimed to your recording afterward.
- Use a quiet room and keep the microphone at a consistent distance. Record a short sample first and listen for background noise, clipping and words that are difficult to hear. Avoid recording music or sound effects with your voice.
- Leave about two seconds of quiet at the start and end, and a short natural pause between sections. If you make a mistake, pause and repeat the complete sentence; note the retake when you send the file so it can be removed.
- WAV is preferred: mono, 48 kHz, 16-bit or 24-bit if your recorder offers those settings. A high-quality M4A or MP3 from a phone or recorder is also suitable; choose the highest practical quality. If MP3 quality is selectable, use at least 192 kbps. Do not convert an existing compressed recording to WAV just to change its extension.
- Send the five original audio files together and mention any intentional wording changes, omissions or retakes. Keep your own copy. No backing music, effects, normalization or video editing is required before sending them.
