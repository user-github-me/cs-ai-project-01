There is no universally best architecture
Choose a design for its task, operating conditions and consequences of failure. A model with
excellent offline accuracy may be unsuitable if it misses a deadline or requires too much memory.
A highly connected system may be fragile if local operation depends on one remote service. A
simple fixed-rule controller can be appropriate for a well-defined task.
Make the comparison explicit
State the intended outcome, identify constraints, compare options and define measures for
success. Evaluate the combination of hardware, networking, algorithms and human operation. A
component’s specification is useful evidence, but it is not proof that the complete application
meets its requirements.

Reliable decisions need reliable inputs
Sensors are imperfect
Noise creates random variation; drift changes a sensor’s behaviour over time. Obstruction,
calibration error or environmental conditions can produce misleading observations. Sensor fusion
combines information from multiple sources, often after time alignment and filtering. Different
sensors can have complementary strengths, but they can also share common failure causes.

Do not combine incompatible units or measurements from different times as if they described the same
instant.
Define reliability under conditions
Reliability concerns performing an intended function for a stated period under stated conditions.
MTBF is a statistical measure for repairable systems, not a promise that a particular unit cannot fail
earlier. Monitor drift, error rates, temperature and data quality. Redundancy can help when
failures are sufficiently independent and the system can recognise and manage disagreement.

Capacity is only one part of scaling
Adding hundreds of IoT devices can increase addressing, authentication, message traffic, storage
and maintenance work. Agree on data formats, units, timestamps and interface versions as well as
the physical link. Two products using Wi-Fi do not automatically interpret one another’s messages
correctly.
Plan for failures
Redundant links, retry policies, local buffering and controlled reconnection can improve resilience.
A mesh can route around some failures when an alternative path exists and the protocols support
it. It does not guarantee communication through every outage. Test overload and partial failure,
not just the normal case with every device online.

An illustrative warehouse design
Each robot handles immediate motion control locally. A shared service allocates tasks and

The approaches can overlap
A behavioural model can be used to make predictions, and predictive features can inform an
action policy. They are not mutually exclusive categories defined by one kind of data. Choose the
model and evaluation method based on the question being asked and the evidence available.
Adaptation is not guaranteed generalisation
Adaptive intelligence describes changing behaviour appropriately using new information or
experience. Autonomy describes operating with limited direct human control. An autonomous
device may use a fixed policy; an adaptive model may still need supervision. Performance in
familiar conditions does not prove reliability in a new environment.

Understand why a policy acted
Explainability is a practical capability
Explainability concerns making relevant aspects of a model’s behaviour understandable to
people. A small rule set or Q table may be inspectable. A deep RL policy can be harder to
interpret because many parameters contribute to each output. Complexity does not mean analysis
is impossible, but a simple story about one decision may be incomplete or misleading.
Look beyond one successful outcome
Inspect representative cases, counterexamples, changed conditions and failure logs. Separate a
faithful explanation of the computation from a plausible narrative generated after the fact. An
explanation does not establish that the action was correct; validation requires evidence about
outcomes and requirements.
Reward errors and bias
An agent rewarded only for speed may neglect an important constraint. Training environments
can under-represent some conditions or groups. Distribution shift can reduce performance when
deployment differs from training. Track both overall performance and relevant subsets. Correcting
a reward or dataset requires re-evaluation of the resulting policy.

Physical effects raise the stakes
A connected controller can change the physical world, so a corrupted measurement or
unauthorised command may have consequences beyond a damaged file. Protect sensor inputs,
model updates and actuator commands according to the task. Test how the system responds to
missing, stale and contradictory data.
Finish with a justified judgement
State the benefit you expect, the evidence supporting it, the conditions under which it applies and
the remaining limitations. Decide what a human must monitor or approve and how the system
should respond when its assumptions fail. The strongest design is the one suited to its purpose,
with limitations that are understood and managed.
