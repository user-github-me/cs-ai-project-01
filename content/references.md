# References and attribution

These sources support the facts, dates and case studies used in the six chapters (`chapter-0N/draft.md`) and in the
matching Study Guide V2. They are listed by chapter, in the order the claims appear. External links are for further
reading only — every explanation, activity and answer needed to learn the module is in the chapter files themselves.

## Scope source

- The team's six chapter outlines ("Bone Structures", `drafts/stage 2/Bone Structures/*.pdf`) set the scope, subtopic
  numbering and end-of-chapter questions. Their footers credit *Cambridge International AS & A Level Computer Science*;
  the edition and authors were not recorded by the team (see "Known attribution gaps" below). The chapter exercises in
  `chapter-0N/exercises.md` are the team's own question sets, many in the style of that coursebook.

## Chapter 4 — Networking

- Berners-Lee, T. (1989). *Information Management: A Proposal*. CERN (origin of the World Wide Web).
- ICANN (2011, 3 February). *Available pool of unallocated IPv4 Internet addresses now completely emptied* [announcement].
- Internet Engineering Task Force RFCs: RFC 791 (IPv4, 1981); RFC 8200 (IPv6, 2017); RFC 5952 (rules for writing IPv6
  addresses, 2010); RFC 3849 and RFC 5737 (documentation-only addresses such as 2001:db8::/32 and 192.0.2.0/24);
  RFC 1034/1035 (DNS, 1987); RFC 3986 (URI/URL syntax, 2005); RFC 9110 (HTTP semantics, 2022); RFC 959 (FTP, 1985);
  RFC 5321 (SMTP, 2008); RFC 1939 (POP3, 1996); RFC 9051 (IMAP4rev2, 2021); RFC 8446 (TLS 1.3, 2018);
  RFC 5280 (X.509 digital certificates, 2008).
- IEEE 802.3 (Ethernet, including CSMA/CD) and IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi, including CSMA/CA) standards.
- Bennett, C. H., & Brassard, G. (1984). Quantum cryptography: Public key distribution and coin tossing. *Proceedings of
  the IEEE International Conference on Computers, Systems and Signal Processing*, 175–179 (the BB84 QKD protocol).
