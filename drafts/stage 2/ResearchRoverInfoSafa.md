## Hardware:

	The lander’s main tasks were to image the rover’s operations and sending data from the rover to Earth. 
	The lander had rechargeable batteries and over 2.5 m of solar cells on its petals.
	Hardware included a steerable antenna, gallium-arsenide solar arrays that generated enough power to transmit for 2–4 hours per sol and maintain 128 megabytes of dynamic memory through the night.
	The lander contained a stereoscopic camera with spatial filters on an expandable pole called Imager for Mars Pathfinder (IMP), and the Atmospheric Structure Instrument/Meteorology Package (ASI/MET) which acted as a Mars meteorological station, collecting data about pressure, temperature, and winds. 
	The rover's wheels were made of aluminum. Each wheel was driven by its own independent motor.
	The wheels were connected to a specific suspension system to ensure contact with the ground no matter how bumpy the terrain was.
	A six-wheeled model was preferred over the four-wheeled one because it reduced the pressure on each wheel and allowed obstacles to be overcome.
	Sojourner's central processing unit (CPU) was an Intel 80C85 with a 2 MHz clock, addressing 64 kilobytes (Kb) of memory.
	 64 Kb of RAM for the main processor, 16 Kb of PROM, 176 Kb of non-volatile storage, and 512 Kb of temporary data storage.
	The rover had two monochrome cameras in front and a color camera at the rear. Each front camera had an array 484 pixels high by 768 wide. 
	Both front cameras were coupled with five laser stripe projectors that enabled stereoscopic images to be taken along with measurements for hazard detection in the rover's path.
